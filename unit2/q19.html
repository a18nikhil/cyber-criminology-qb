Q19. Describe Cyber-Routine Activities Theory, Its Three Main Components, and How It Explains Patterns of Online Victimization.

Introduction

Cyber-Routine Activities Theory explains how people's regular online activities can increase or decrease their risk of becoming victims of cybercrime.

According to this theory, cybercrime becomes more likely when three conditions come together: a motivated offender, a suitable target and lack of capable guardianship.

1. Three Main Components of the Theory

a) Motivated Offender

A motivated offender is a person who is willing to commit cybercrime.

Hackers, online scammers, cyberstalkers and identity thieves may search the internet for possible victims.

Example: A scammer searching social media for people to target with fake messages.

b) Suitable Target

A suitable target is a person, account or device that appears easy or valuable to attack.

Weak passwords, public personal information and unsafe online behaviour can make someone a suitable target.

Example: A public social media account containing personal details may attract online scammers.

c) Lack of Capable Guardianship

Capable guardianship means protection that helps prevent cybercrime.

Weak privacy settings, outdated software, lack of two-factor authentication and poor security awareness can reduce protection.

Example: An account without strong security protection may be easier for an offender to access.

2. How the Theory Explains Online Victimization

The theory suggests that online victimization becomes more likely when a motivated offender finds a suitable target with weak protection.

Regular online activities can influence this risk in the following ways:

  1. Sharing Personal Information: Publicly sharing personal details may attract scammers or identity thieves.
  2. Communicating with Strangers: Interacting with unknown people may increase exposure to online fraud or harassment.
  3. Unsafe Downloading: Downloading files from unknown sources may expose users to malware.
  4. Weak Security Practices: Using weak passwords or ignoring security updates may make accounts easier to attack.

Example: A person publicly shares personal details and uses a weak password. A scammer finds the account, and the lack of strong security increases the risk of account hacking or identity theft.

Conclusion

Cyber-Routine Activities Theory explains online victimization through a motivated offender, a suitable target and lack of capable guardianship. When these three conditions come together, the risk of cybercrime victimization increases.
