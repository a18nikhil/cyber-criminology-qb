Q27. Discuss Cyber-Routine Activities Theory and Its Application to Online Victimization.

Introduction

Cyber-Routine Activities Theory explains how a person's regular online activities can affect their chances of becoming a victim of cybercrime.

The theory says that cyber victimization becomes more likely when three conditions come together:

  1. Motivated offender
  2. Suitable target
  3. Lack of capable guardianship

1. Motivated Offender

A motivated offender is someone who is willing to commit cybercrime.

Examples include:

  • Hackers
  • Online scammers
  • Cyberstalkers
  • Identity thieves
  • Other online offenders

The internet allows such offenders to search for possible victims without being physically near them.

2. Suitable Target

A suitable target is a person, account, device or organization that appears easy or valuable to attack.

A person may become a suitable target by:

  • Using weak passwords
  • Sharing personal information publicly
  • Accepting requests from strangers
  • Clicking suspicious links
  • Visiting unsafe websites
  • Keeping social media accounts public

3. Lack of Capable Guardianship

Capable guardianship means having protection against cyber threats.

Lack of protection may include:

  • No antivirus or security software
  • Weak privacy settings
  • No two-factor authentication
  • Outdated software
  • Poor knowledge of cyber threats
  • Lack of proper online monitoring

Weak protection makes it easier for offenders to attack.

4. Application to Online Victimization

The theory can be applied to different types of online victimization.

Online Activity Possible Victimization
Sharing personal information publicly Identity theft or stalking
Accepting unknown friend requests Harassment or fraud
Clicking suspicious links Phishing or malware
Using weak passwords Account hacking
Downloading unknown files Malware infection
Shopping on unsafe websites Financial fraud

5. Example of the Theory

Suppose a student keeps a social media profile public and shares personal details online.

An online scammer finds the profile and sends a fake link. The student has weak security and clicks the link.

Here:

Scammer: Motivated Offender

Student's account: Suitable Target

Weak security: Lack of Capable Guardianship

This may result in account theft, fraud or loss of personal information.

6. Application in Reducing Victimization

The theory also shows how cyber victimization can be reduced.

Users can:

  • Use strong and different passwords.
  • Enable two-factor authentication.
  • Keep social media accounts private.
  • Avoid suspicious links and files.
  • Avoid sharing unnecessary personal information.
  • Keep devices and software updated.
  • Use trusted security software.

7. Simple Flow

Regular Online Activities
↓
Exposure to Online Offenders
↓
Suitable Target + Weak Protection
↓
Higher Risk of Online Victimization

Conclusion

Cyber-Routine Activities Theory shows that online victimization depends on the interaction between motivated offenders, suitable targets and lack of capable guardianship. It can be applied to cyberspace to explain why certain online activities increase the risk of hacking, fraud, stalking, identity theft and other forms of cyber victimization.
