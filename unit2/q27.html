Q27. Discuss Cyber-Routine Activities Theory and Its Application to Online Victimization.

Introduction

Cyber-Routine Activities Theory explains how people's regular online activities can affect their risk of becoming victims of cybercrime.

According to this theory, online victimization becomes more likely when three conditions come together: a motivated offender, a suitable target and lack of capable guardianship.

1. Three Main Components of the Theory

a) Motivated Offender

A motivated offender is a person who is willing to commit cybercrime.

Hackers, online scammers, cyberstalkers and identity thieves may search the internet for possible victims.

b) Suitable Target

A suitable target is a person, account or device that appears easy or valuable to attack.

Public personal information, weak passwords and unsafe online behaviour can make someone a suitable target.

c) Lack of Capable Guardianship

Capable guardianship means protection that helps prevent cybercrime.

Weak privacy settings, outdated software, lack of two-factor authentication and poor security awareness can reduce protection.

2. Application to Online Victimization

The theory explains how different online activities can increase the risk of cybercrime victimization.

Online Activity Possible Victimization
Sharing personal information publicly Identity theft or stalking
Accepting unknown friend requests Online harassment or fraud
Clicking suspicious links Phishing or malware infection
Using weak passwords Account hacking
Downloading files from unknown sources Malware infection
Shopping on unsafe websites Financial fraud

3. Example of the Theory

Suppose a student keeps their social media profile public and shares personal details online.

An online scammer finds the profile and sends a fake link. The student clicks the link without checking whether it is safe.

In this example:

  • Motivated Offender: The online scammer.
  • Suitable Target: The student's account.
  • Lack of Capable Guardianship: Poor security awareness and failure to check the suspicious link.

These conditions may increase the risk of account theft or online fraud.

Conclusion

Cyber-Routine Activities Theory explains online victimization through motivated offenders, suitable targets and lack of capable guardianship. It helps explain why certain online activities increase the risk of hacking, fraud, stalking and identity theft.
