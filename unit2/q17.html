Q17. List and Explain Any Three Criminological Predictors of Digital Piracy.

Introduction

Digital piracy means illegally copying, downloading or sharing copyrighted content such as movies, music, software and games.

Criminological predictors are factors that help explain why people may become involved in digital piracy.

1. Peer Influence

Peer influence means the influence of friends or other people in a group.

  • Friends may encourage a person to download or share pirated content.
  • When piracy is common among friends, it may seem normal and acceptable.
  • A person may copy the behaviour of others to fit into the group.

Example: A student may start downloading pirated movies because their friends regularly do so.

2. Low Self-Control

Low self-control means difficulty controlling one's actions and thinking about future consequences.

  • A person may choose pirated content because it is free and easily available.
  • They may want quick access to movies, music or games without paying.
  • They may ignore the legal consequences of piracy.

Example: A person may download a pirated game immediately instead of purchasing a legal copy.

3. Neutralization

Neutralization means giving excuses to make illegal behaviour seem acceptable.

People involved in digital piracy may think:

  • "Everyone downloads pirated content."
  • "No one is really getting hurt."
  • "The original product is too expensive."
  • "Big companies already earn enough money."

Such excuses may reduce feelings of guilt and encourage continued piracy.

Conclusion

Three important criminological predictors of digital piracy are peer influence, low self-control and neutralization. Peer influence makes piracy seem normal, low self-control encourages quick decisions, and neutralization helps people justify illegal behaviour.
