Q1. Explain Bandura's Theory of Reciprocal Determinism and Its Application to Cyber-Criminal Behavior.

Introduction

Bandura's Theory of Reciprocal Determinism explains that human behaviour is influenced by three factors:

  • Personal Factors
  • Behaviour
  • Environment

According to psychologist Albert Bandura, these three factors continuously influence one another.

1. Three Main Factors of Reciprocal Determinism

a) Personal Factors

Personal factors include a person's thoughts, feelings, beliefs and motivations.

Example: A person may believe that online fraud is an easy way to earn money.

b) Behaviour

Behaviour means the actions performed by a person.

Example: Online fraud, illegal hacking, cyberbullying or digital piracy.

c) Environment

Environment includes the people and situations around a person.

In cyberspace, it may include friends, social media, online communities and hacker groups.

2. Reciprocal Relationship

The three factors influence one another.

For example, a person's beliefs may influence their online behaviour. Their behaviour may change their environment, and the environment may again influence their beliefs.

This continuous interaction is called reciprocal determinism.

3. Application to Cyber-Criminal Behavior

Bandura's theory helps explain how a person may become involved in cybercrime.

a) Personal Motivation

A person may commit cybercrime because of the desire for money, curiosity, revenge or recognition.

b) Influence of Online Environment

Online groups may encourage cybercrime. If group members support illegal activities, a person may begin to think such behaviour is acceptable.

c) Learning from Others

People may learn cyber-criminal behaviour by watching and copying others.

Example: A person may see friends earning money through online fraud and try to copy them.

d) Rewards Encourage Repetition

If a person receives money, praise or recognition from cybercrime, they may repeat the behaviour.

Conclusion

Bandura's Theory of Reciprocal Determinism explains that personal factors, behaviour and environment continuously influence one another. In cybercrime, personal motivations, online groups, learning from others and rewards can influence a person's criminal behaviour.
