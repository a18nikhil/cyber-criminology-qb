Q25. Compare Yahoo Boyism with other forms of cyber-fraud subcultures in terms of social and economic drivers.

Introduction

Yahoo Boyism is a cyber-fraud subculture mainly associated with online fraudsters in Nigeria. Yahoo Boys use the internet to cheat victims and earn money.

Like other cyber-fraud groups, Yahoo Boyism is influenced by both social drivers and economic drivers.

  • Social drivers: Social conditions that may influence a person, such as friends, family, social status and peer pressure.
  • Economic drivers: Money-related conditions, such as unemployment, poverty and the desire to earn quick money.

1. Social Drivers of Yahoo Boyism

Some important social drivers are:

  1. Peer Influence: A person may enter online fraud because friends or other people around them are already involved in it.
  2. Desire for Social Status: Expensive clothes, cars and a rich lifestyle may be seen as signs of success. This can attract some young people towards cyber-fraud.
  3. Learning from Others: New offenders may learn fraud methods from experienced fraudsters.
  4. Online Groups: Social media and online groups make it easier for fraudsters to communicate and share information.
  5. Social Recognition: Successful fraudsters may receive attention or respect from some people around them, which can encourage others to copy them.

2. Economic Drivers of Yahoo Boyism

Economic conditions can also encourage some people to enter cyber-fraud.

  1. Unemployment: Lack of jobs may lead some people to look for illegal ways of earning money.
  2. Poverty: Financial problems can make quick money from online fraud appear attractive.
  3. Desire for Quick Money: Cyber-fraud may be seen as an easy way to earn large amounts of money in a short time.
  4. Low Cost of Online Fraud: A smartphone, computer and internet connection may be enough to start some types of online scams.
  5. Large Number of Online Victims: The internet allows fraudsters to contact people from different countries, increasing their chances of getting money.

Conclusion

Yahoo Boyism and other cyber-fraud subcultures have many similar social and economic drivers. Peer influence, desire for social status, unemployment, financial problems and the attraction of quick money can encourage cyber-fraud. The main difference is that Yahoo Boyism developed in the Nigerian social and economic setting, while other cyber-fraud subcultures may develop in different countries and social conditions.
