Q25. Compare Yahoo Boyism with Other Forms of Cyber-Fraud Subcultures in Terms of Social and Economic Drivers.

Introduction

Yahoo Boyism is a form of online fraud mainly associated with Nigeria. Yahoo Boys use the internet to deceive people and obtain money illegally.

Like other cyber-fraud groups, Yahoo Boyism is influenced by social and economic drivers.

  • Social drivers: Influence of friends, groups and society.
  • Economic drivers: Factors such as poverty, unemployment and the desire for quick money.

1. Comparison of Social Drivers

Social Driver Yahoo Boyism Other Cyber-Fraud Groups
Peer Influence Friends may encourage others to join online fraud. Online criminal groups may encourage others to commit fraud.
Social Status Expensive clothes, cars and a rich lifestyle may attract young people. Money, success and reputation may attract offenders.
Learning from Others New members may learn fraud methods from experienced Yahoo Boys. Members may learn fraud methods from other online criminals.
Group Recognition Successful Yahoo Boys may receive respect from their groups. Successful offenders may also receive respect from their groups.

2. Comparison of Economic Drivers

Economic Driver Yahoo Boyism Other Cyber-Fraud Groups
Unemployment Lack of jobs may encourage some young people to commit fraud. Lack of jobs may also encourage fraud in other countries.
Poverty Financial problems may push some people towards online fraud. Financial problems may also encourage other offenders.
Quick Money Some people commit fraud to become rich quickly. Other offenders may also want to earn money quickly.
Low Cost Cyber cafés historically provided affordable internet access. Internet access and digital tools can make online fraud inexpensive.

Conclusion

Yahoo Boyism and other cyber-fraud groups share similar social and economic drivers, including peer influence, social status, unemployment, poverty and the desire for quick money. However, Yahoo Boyism is particularly associated with Nigeria's social and economic conditions, while other cyber-fraud subcultures develop in different environments.
