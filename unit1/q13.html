Q13. Explain the Term Internet Child Pornography Consumers with Reference to Their Online Patterns and the Psychological Factors That Drive Such Behavior.

Introduction

Internet Child Pornography Consumers are people who use the internet to search for, view, download, store, or share illegal sexual material involving children. A more current term for such material is Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) because it makes clear that the material records the sexual abuse or exploitation of a child.

Their behaviour can be understood by studying their online patterns and the psychological factors that may drive such behaviour.

1. Online Patterns of Consumers

Some common online patterns include:

  1. Searching for illegal material: They may repeatedly search online for sexual material involving children.
  2. Downloading and storing: Some consumers download and keep illegal images or videos on digital devices.
  3. Repeated viewing: The material may be viewed repeatedly over a period of time.
  4. Sharing or exchanging: Some offenders may exchange illegal material with other offenders through online networks or groups.
  5. Using hidden identities: Fake usernames or accounts may be used to hide their real identity.
  6. Joining online communities: Some offenders may communicate with others who have similar illegal interests.
  7. Keeping activities secret: They may hide files, accounts or online activities because they know the behaviour is illegal.

2. Psychological Factors

Several psychological factors may help explain why some offenders continue this behaviour.

a) Sexual Interest in Children

Some offenders may have a sexual interest in children, which can lead them to seek such illegal material.

b) Curiosity

For some individuals, the behaviour may begin with curiosity and later develop into repeated viewing.

c) Habit and Repetition

Repeated use may develop into a strong habit, making the person continue seeking similar material.

d) Feeling of Anonymity

The internet can create a feeling that a person is hidden or cannot be identified. This may reduce fear and encourage illegal behaviour.

e) Online Group Influence

Interaction with other offenders may make the behaviour seem more acceptable within that group and encourage continued consumption.

f) Lack of Self-Control

Some offenders may have difficulty controlling their urges or stopping their online behaviour even when they understand its harmful and illegal nature.

3. Relationship Between Online Patterns and Psychological Factors

The relationship can be shown as:

4. Harmful and Criminal Nature

This behaviour is serious because the material involves the sexual abuse and exploitation of children. Viewing, possessing, distributing or producing such material can also be a criminal offence under applicable law.

Demand for such material can contribute to the continued exploitation of children and can cause long-lasting harm to victims.

Conclusion

Internet Child Pornography Consumers are individuals who use the internet to consume illegal sexual material involving children. Their online patterns may include searching, repeated viewing, downloading, storing and sharing, while factors such as sexual interest, curiosity, habit, perceived anonymity, group influence and poor self-control may contribute to the behaviour. Such activity is harmful because it is connected with the sexual exploitation and abuse of children.
