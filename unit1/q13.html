Q13. Explain the Term Internet Child Pornography Consumers with Reference to Their Online Patterns and the Psychological Factors That Drive Such Behavior.

Introduction

Internet Child Pornography Consumers are people who use the internet to search for, view, download or share sexual abuse material involving children.

This material is also called Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) because it involves the sexual abuse or exploitation of children.

Their behaviour can be understood through their online patterns and psychological factors.

1. Online Patterns of Consumers

Common online patterns may include:

  1. Searching for Illegal Material: They may search online for sexual abuse material involving children.
  2. Downloading and Storing: Some offenders download and keep illegal images or videos on their devices.
  3. Repeated Viewing: They may view such material repeatedly over time.
  4. Sharing or Exchanging: Some offenders share illegal material with others through online networks or groups.
  5. Hidden Identities: They may use fake usernames or accounts to hide their identity and activities.
  6. Online Communities: Some offenders communicate with others who have similar illegal interests.

2. Psychological Factors

Psychological factors are related to a person's thoughts, feelings and motivations.

  1. Sexual Interest in Children: Some offenders may have a sexual interest in children, which can lead them to seek such illegal material.
  2. Curiosity: For some individuals, the behaviour may begin with curiosity and later develop into repeated viewing.
  3. Habit and Repetition: Repeated viewing may become a strong habit, making it difficult for some offenders to stop.
  4. Feeling of Anonymity: Some offenders believe their online identity is hidden, which may reduce their fear of being caught.
  5. Group Influence: Contact with other offenders may make the behaviour seem acceptable within those groups.
  6. Poor Self-Control: Some offenders may find it difficult to control their behaviour even when they understand that it is harmful and illegal.

Conclusion

Internet Child Pornography Consumers use the internet to access illegal sexual abuse material involving children. Their online patterns may include searching, downloading, repeated viewing and sharing. Psychological factors such as sexual interest, curiosity, habit, perceived anonymity, group influence and poor self-control may contribute to this behaviour.
