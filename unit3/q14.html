Q14. Discuss Cyber Vandalism as a Form of Cybercrime, Covering Its Motivations and Consequences.

Introduction

Cyber vandalism is a form of cybercrime in which a person intentionally damages, changes, destroys or disrupts digital systems, websites, data or online services without permission.

Examples include changing website content, deleting important files and disrupting online services.

1. Motivations Behind Cyber Vandalism

a) Fun or Excitement

Some offenders damage websites or systems for entertainment, curiosity or the excitement of causing trouble.

b) Revenge

Offenders may damage digital property because of anger, personal conflict or dissatisfaction.

Example: A former employee deliberately deletes important company files.

c) Recognition and Showing Technical Skills

Some offenders attack systems to demonstrate their technical abilities, gain attention or earn recognition from online groups.

d) Political or Social Reasons

Offenders may change or damage websites to express political opinions, protest against organizations or spread social messages.

2. Consequences of Cyber Vandalism

a) Financial Loss

Organizations may spend money repairing damaged systems, restoring files and improving security.

b) Loss of Data

Important files or information may be changed, damaged or permanently deleted.

c) Disruption of Services

Websites, networks and online services may stop working, affecting users and organizations.

d) Damage to Reputation

Cyber vandalism may reduce public trust in an organization when its website or services are damaged.

e) Privacy and Security Risks

Attacks may expose sensitive information or create weaknesses that lead to further security problems.

f) Legal Consequences

Offenders may face legal action, fines or other penalties depending on the offence and applicable laws.

Conclusion

Cyber vandalism involves intentionally damaging or disrupting digital property. It may be motivated by excitement, revenge, recognition or political reasons. Its consequences include financial loss, data damage, service disruption, reputation damage and legal penalties.
