Q8. Describe Malware Victimization with Examples, and Explain the Common Ways in Which Individuals and Organizations Become Victims.

Introduction

Malware means malicious software designed to damage devices, steal information or gain unauthorized access to computer systems.

Malware victimization occurs when individuals or organizations suffer harm because their devices or systems become infected with malware.

1. Common Types of Malware with Examples

a) Virus

A virus attaches itself to files or programs and spreads when infected files are executed.

Example: A virus damages important files on a computer.

b) Worm

A worm spreads automatically between computers through networks.

Example: A worm spreads across an organization's network and affects several computers.

c) Trojan

A Trojan is harmful software disguised as a genuine or useful program.

Example: A fake application secretly installs malware on a user's device.

d) Ransomware

Ransomware locks or encrypts files and demands payment to restore access.

Example: A company's files are encrypted, and attackers demand money to unlock them.

e) Spyware

Spyware secretly monitors activities or collects personal information.

Example: Spyware steals passwords or banking details.

2. Common Ways Individuals Become Victims

  • Phishing Links: Clicking harmful links received through emails or messages.
  • Infected Attachments: Opening email attachments containing malware.
  • Unsafe Downloads: Downloading files or software from untrusted websites.
  • Pirated Software: Installing cracked software that contains hidden malware.
  • Infected Devices: Connecting infected USB drives to computers.

3. Common Ways Organizations Become Victims

a) Phishing Emails

Employees may open fake emails containing malicious links or attachments.

b) Weak Security

Weak passwords and poor security settings may allow attackers to gain access and install malware.

c) Outdated Software

Attackers may exploit security weaknesses in software that has not been updated.

d) Infected Files or Devices

Malware may enter company systems through infected downloads or USB drives.

e) Human Error

Employees may accidentally install unsafe programs or allow malicious software to run.

Conclusion

Malware victimization occurs when malicious software harms individuals or organizations. Viruses, worms, Trojans, ransomware and spyware are common examples. Victims may become infected through phishing, unsafe downloads, infected devices, weak security and outdated software.
