Q24. Explain Any Two Legal Provisions Addressing Cybercrime Victimization, Along with Their Scope and Limitations in Protecting Victims.

Introduction

Cybercrime laws help protect people from offences such as hacking, identity theft, online fraud, privacy violations and the misuse of personal information.

In India, the Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000 contains important provisions dealing with cyber offences. Two useful provisions are Section 66C and Section 66D.

1. Section 66C – Identity Theft

Section 66C of the IT Act, 2000 deals with identity theft.

Identity theft occurs when a person dishonestly or fraudulently uses another person's:

  • Electronic signature
  • Password
  • Unique identification feature

Example: A criminal steals another person's login details and uses them to access an online account.

Scope

This provision helps protect victims against the misuse of their digital identity.

It is useful in cases involving:

  • Stolen passwords
  • Misuse of login credentials
  • Unauthorized use of digital identity
  • Other forms of online identity theft

Limitation

  • Identifying the real offender may be difficult when fake identities or hidden accounts are used.
  • Victims may not immediately know that their identity has been stolen.
  • Investigation may become difficult when offenders operate from different locations or countries.

2. Section 66D – Cheating by Personation Using Computer Resources

Section 66D of the IT Act, 2000 deals with cheating by pretending to be another person through a computer or communication device.

Example: A fraudster creates a fake bank profile, pretends to be a bank employee and tricks a victim into giving sensitive information or money.

Scope

This provision can apply to online cheating involving:

  • Fake identities
  • Impersonation
  • Online scams
  • Fraudulent messages
  • Fake profiles used for cheating

It helps protect victims from people who use digital technology to deceive them by pretending to be someone else.

Limitation

  • Cybercriminals can use fake accounts and false information to hide their identity.
  • Offenders may operate from outside the victim's state or country.
  • Victims may have difficulty collecting and preserving digital evidence.
  • Legal action may take time because cybercrime investigations can involve several digital platforms.

3. Comparison of the Two Provisions

Legal Provision Main Purpose Example
Section 66C Deals with identity theft Using another person's stolen password
Section 66D Deals with cheating by online impersonation Pretending to be a bank employee to commit fraud

4. Importance for Cybercrime Victims

These provisions help victims by:

  • Recognizing identity theft and online impersonation as punishable offences.
  • Providing a legal basis for taking action against offenders.
  • Protecting digital identity and online transactions.
  • Helping authorities investigate cyber fraud and identity-related offences.

However, laws alone cannot completely prevent cybercrime. Anonymity, cross-border offences, difficulty in identifying offenders and problems with digital evidence can limit their effectiveness.

Conclusion

Sections 66C and 66D of the Information Technology Act, 2000 are important provisions for protecting cybercrime victims in India. Section 66C deals with identity theft, while Section 66D deals with cheating through online impersonation. They provide legal protection to victims, but practical problems such as hidden identities, cross-border crimes and difficulty in investigation can limit their effectiveness.
