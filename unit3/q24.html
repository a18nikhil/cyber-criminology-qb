Q24. Explain Any Two Legal Provisions Addressing Cybercrime Victimization, Along with Their Scope and Limitations in Protecting Victims.

Introduction

Cybercrime laws help protect people from online crimes such as identity theft, fraud and misuse of personal information.

In India, the Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000 contains important provisions to deal with cybercrime. Two such provisions are Section 66C and Section 66D.

1. Section 66C – Identity Theft

Section 66C deals with the dishonest or fraudulent use of another person's password, electronic signature or unique identification details.

Example: A criminal steals someone's password and uses it to access their online account.

Scope:

  • Protects people against the misuse of their digital identity.
  • Covers offences involving stolen passwords and identification details.
  • Provides punishment of up to 3 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to ₹1 lakh.

Limitations:

  • Offenders using fake identities may be difficult to identify.
  • Victims may discover identity theft late, making investigation difficult.

2. Section 66D – Cheating by Online Impersonation

Section 66D deals with cheating by pretending to be another person through a computer or communication device.

Example: A fraudster pretends to be a bank employee and tricks someone into transferring money.

Scope:

  • Protects people against online cheating through impersonation.
  • Covers fraud involving fake identities, fake bank officials and fraudulent online profiles.
  • Provides punishment of up to 3 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to ₹1 lakh.

Limitations:

  • Offenders operating from other countries may be difficult to trace.
  • Collecting digital evidence and completing investigations may take time.

Conclusion

Sections 66C and 66D of the IT Act, 2000 address identity theft and online cheating through impersonation. They provide legal protection to cybercrime victims, but hidden identities, cross-border crimes and investigation difficulties can limit their effectiveness.
