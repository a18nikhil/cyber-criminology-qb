Q10. Describe Cyberbullying and Its Impact on Victims.

Introduction

Cyberbullying means using the internet or digital technology to repeatedly harass, insult, threaten, embarrass or harm another person.

It can occur through social media, messaging apps, emails, online games and other digital platforms. Unlike traditional bullying, it can happen at any time, and harmful content can spread quickly.

1. Common Forms of Cyberbullying

  1. Abusive Messages: Sending repeated insulting, offensive or threatening messages.
  2. Spreading Rumours: Sharing false information to damage someone's reputation.
  3. Online Humiliation: Posting embarrassing comments, photographs or videos to publicly humiliate someone.
  4. Impersonation: Using fake accounts or another person's identity to post harmful content.
  5. Online Exclusion: Intentionally excluding someone from online groups or activities to make them feel isolated.

2. Impact of Cyberbullying on Victims

a) Emotional Stress and Anxiety

Victims may experience fear, sadness, stress and constant worry about further harassment.

b) Loss of Confidence

Repeated insults and humiliation can reduce a person's self-confidence and self-esteem.

c) Social Isolation

Victims may avoid friends, social media or social activities because they feel embarrassed or unsafe.

d) Educational Problems

Students may find it difficult to concentrate on studies, affecting their academic performance.

e) Sleep Problems

Fear and stress caused by cyberbullying may disturb sleep and affect daily activities.

f) Damage to Reputation

False rumours, embarrassing images and harmful posts may damage a victim's reputation.

Conclusion

Cyberbullying involves repeated online harassment, threats or humiliation. It can seriously affect victims by causing emotional stress, loss of confidence, social isolation, educational difficulties and damage to reputation.
